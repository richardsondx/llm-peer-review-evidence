Literature audit: 27 studies of AI peer review

A source-based reassessment of 27 studies, using newer models to check the evidence behind an earlier review.

By Richardson Dackam · Independent researcher, Nshipyard
1 October 2026 · Version 0.2.0 · Research report · Not peer reviewed

Three versions available: original baseline, Luna and Sol.

Both newer-model extractions now cover all 27 studies. Compare every finding side by side, with targeted corrections checked against primary sources. This is an AI-assisted evidence reassessment, not a new experiment evaluating Luna or Sol as peer reviewers.

Why revisit the evidence?

I wanted to verify whether the findings of a previous review still hold when newer models independently examine the same studies. The goal is to check the interpretation of existing evidence, identify extraction errors, and make each finding traceable to its primary source.

GPT-6-Luna and GPT-6.1-Sol act as research assistants in this reassessment. The studies themselves evaluate the models and systems described in their original experiments. A finding about GPT-4 is a historical result from a paper, not the identity of the model that extracted it here.

What the completed extraction tells us

The Luna evidence record distinguishes useful feedback and overlap with human comments from detection of verified scientific errors. It records score leniency in several designs and mixed error-detection performance. Different tasks, samples and denominators prevent a single pooled estimate.

The broad concerns about reviewer reliability, score leniency and missed errors remain relevant, but the baseline’s blanket “under half of real flaws” claim requires correction. Synthetic-error and real-error benchmarks are distinct; some reported rates exceed half. Direct confidence calibration is also distinct from unsupported criticism or false alarms.

Methods you can inspect

The same candidate list, six dimensions and source-version rules were supplied to both agents. Sol extracted without inspecting Luna’s findings. A supervising AI agent checked structure and sampled source claims and corrected identified errors. Every cell carries an evidence status; a missing measurement is not a negative result.

This is a fixed-corpus reassessment, not a new exhaustive literature search or a registered systematic review. The source cutoff is 1 October 2026.
